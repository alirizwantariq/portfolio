03 Approach How I get to the bottom of things

  1. Start at the failure, not the feature

    Before I write anything I want to know what it looks like when this breaks and who pays for it. On fraud systems that question orders every decision after it — false positives and false negatives cost completely different people completely different things.

  2. Measure, then optimise — in that order

    "It feels slow" isn't a bug report. Getting ingestion 150% faster started with finding out where the time actually went, which was not where the team assumed it was.

  3. Ship the boring version first

    A working baseline in production teaches you more in a week than a month of speculation. I'd rather deploy a simple model, watch it fail honestly, and earn the complexity than defend an elegant thing nobody has stress-tested.

  4. Assume someone is trying to break it

    I audit my own architectures for logic flaws and exploits before they reach production. The camera injection vulnerability at Facia wasn't found by a scanner — it was found by asking what I'd do if I wanted to get past it.
